Anatolian Crossroads

A Memory Graph from Neolithic Symbols to Iron Age Inscriptions

In Anatolia and Upper Mesopotamia, memory is first constructed through stone, body, animal and ritual imagery; it is later institutionalized through seals, tablets, inscriptions, palaces and urban plans. This project models that long arc as a comparative network: one macro atlas spanning ten sites and 9,000 years, and deep per-site modules in which every node and edge cites its sources.

The graphs compare — they never claim continuity. Cross-site relations are restricted to geographic corridors, explicit comparative pairs and shared concepts; interpretive readings live in their own nodes, marked by confidence.
